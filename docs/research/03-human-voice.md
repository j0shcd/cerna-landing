## 1. Twelve distilled principles

*The rules and invented do/don’t examples below are inference from the linked sources.*

1. **Use ordinary language for serious ideas.** Why: Graham argues that simpler prose leaves readers more attention for the idea, including readers whose first language isn’t English. [Write Simply, 2021](https://www.paulgraham.com/simply.html)  
   **Do:** “See where your money went.” **Don’t:** “Unlock financial clarity.”

2. **Write for one person’s ear.** Why: Graham recommends spoken language and reading drafts aloud to catch sentences that sound written. [Write Like You Talk](https://www.paulgraham.com/talk.html)  
   **Do:** “You’ve already done the hard part.” **Don’t:** “For individuals committed to financial intentionality.”

3. **Choose the meaning before the phrase.** Why: Orwell attacks ready-made expressions that substitute for thinking. [Politics and the English Language](https://www.orwellfoundation.com/the-orwell-foundation/orwell/essays-and-other-works/politics-and-the-english-language/)  
   **Do:** “Find transactions counted twice.” **Don’t:** “Bring harmony to your financial landscape.”

4. **Cut redundancy, preserve useful detail.** Why: Strunk’s brevity rule explicitly allows detail when every word contributes. [The Elements of Style](https://www.gutenberg.org/files/37134/37134-h/37134-h.htm)  
   **Do:** “Convert euros to francs.” **Don’t:** “Easily and effortlessly manage currency conversion.”

5. **Explain what your expertise makes you forget.** Why: Pinker identifies assumed shared knowledge as a source of confusing writing. [The Source of Bad Writing](https://stevenpinker.com/files/pinker/files/documents/the-source-of-bad-writing.pdf)  
   **Do:** Explain where data stays. **Don’t:** Expect “local-first” to explain privacy by itself.

6. **Let voice express a real relationship.** Why: Zinsser describes finding his voice when he stopped imitating a persona and wrote as a helpful teacher. [Looking for a Model](https://theamericanscholar.org/looking-for-a-model/)  
   **Do:** Sound like the builder helping someone. **Don’t:** Invent an endlessly cheerful brand character.

7. **Keep voice stable; change tone with circumstances.** Why: Mailchimp separates enduring voice from tone adapted to the reader’s emotional state. [Voice and Tone](https://styleguide.mailchimp.com/voice-and-tone/)  
   **Do:** Warm welcome, plain privacy explanation. **Don’t:** Apply the same cheekiness everywhere.

8. **Make humour optional.** Why: Monzo limits wit by context and explicitly avoids jokes about vulnerable financial situations. [Writing Principles](https://monzo.com/tone-of-voice)  
   **Do:** Gently acknowledge spreadsheet maintenance. **Don’t:** Joke about someone’s spending or financial competence.

9. **Earn authority through usefulness.** Why: GOV.UK combines conversational language with specificity and the authority of someone who can help. [Use the right tone](https://guidance.publishing.service.gov.uk/writing-to-gov-uk-standards/writing-guidelines/right-tone/)  
   **Do:** “Import your bank statement.” **Don’t:** “Please proceed with the ingestion process.”

10. **Describe behaviour before praising quality.** Why: Linear’s changelog explains what changed, what users can do, and relevant conditions. [Changelog](https://linear.app/changelog)  
    **Do:** Name the work Cerna performs. **Don’t:** Call it “powerful”, then leave the mechanism unexplained.

11. **Remove ambiguity where trust depends on it.** Why: Fried and Hansson discuss sacrificing verbal flourish when misunderstanding has consequences. [Principles of Communication, 2024](https://37signals.com/podcast/principles-of-communication/)  
    **Do:** Say what joining the waitlist means. **Don’t:** Imply immediate product access.

12. **Preserve deliberate individuality.** Why: Handley argues that distinctive writing reveals judgment; polishing away every unusual choice can erase it. [What AI Would Delete From Great Writing](https://annhandley.com/what-ai-would-delete-from-great-writing/)  
    **Do:** Keep one accurate, memorable observation. **Don’t:** Insert random quirks to appear human.

## 3. Six ideas most teams miss

*All are inference and proposals for Cerna.*

- **Define what the brand refuses to assume.** Cerna’s starting belief: the reader already cares about money. Avoid remedial language such as “Finally take control.” This extends Monzo’s emphasis on reader focus and avoiding condescension. [Monzo](https://monzo.com/tone-of-voice)

- **Use privacy as a worked explanation.** Show where a statement goes and what happens during categorisation. This applies Pinker’s warning about expert assumptions to “local-first”. Publish only verified behaviour. [Pinker](https://stevenpinker.com/files/pinker/files/documents/the-source-of-bad-writing.pdf)

- **Build voice from decisions, not personality adjectives.** “We explain limits beside the promise” is more actionable than “authentic and empowering”. Stratechery’s explicit commitments offer a useful model. [Thompson](https://stratechery.com/about/)

- **Make restraint a signature.** Try one recognisable observation about maintaining spreadsheets, then use direct language. MUJI offers a philosophical reference for satisfaction without constant escalation. [MUJI](https://www.muji.com/jp/about/en/)

- **Create a protected-detail rule.** Every substantial section needs one detail another product could not inherit unchanged: a statement, duplicate transaction, or currency conversion. Preserve it during editing. This combines Orwell’s specificity with Linear’s demonstrable changes. [Orwell](https://www.orwellfoundation.com/the-orwell-foundation/orwell/essays-and-other-works/politics-and-the-english-language/), [Linear](https://linear.app/changelog)

- **Give the founder a bounded presence.** A short, signed explanation of why Cerna exists can carry more personality than anthropomorphising the app. Borrow the accountability of a named author, without copying an essay’s length. [Zinsser](https://theamericanscholar.org/looking-for-a-model/)

## 4. Failure modes, ban list, and editing checklist

Handley explicitly rejects treating em dashes, triplets, or contrast constructions alone as proof of AI authorship. Use the following as **editorial constraints for Cerna**, not a detector. [AI Slop, Accusations, And A Better Way](https://annhandley.com/ai-slop/)

**Proposed ban list — inference**

| Remove by default | Reason or replacement |
|---|---|
| seamless, effortless, frictionless | Describe the work removed and the effort remaining. |
| unlock, elevate, empower, revolutionise | Name an action or outcome. |
| powerful, intelligent, cutting-edge, next-generation | Show the capability. |
| financial wellness, financial journey, financial freedom | State the narrower benefit Cerna can support. |
| “Not just X, but Y”; “It isn’t X. It’s Y.” | Delete manufactured profundity; explain the actual distinction. |
| Repeated triplets, fragments, em-dash asides | Keep only when they improve meaning or rhythm. |
| “Meet your new financial best friend” | Avoid forced intimacy. |
| “Never worry about money again” | Replace an unlimited emotional promise with a bounded product promise. |

Other failure modes, **inference**: synonym rotation that obscures one concept; equal-length feature cards with interchangeable claims; borrowed irreverence; relentless reassurance unsupported by detail; short sentences chopped into an unnatural drumbeat.

**Line-by-line editor’s checklist — inference**

1. What does this line help the reader understand or do?
2. Who acts, and what happens?
3. Which concrete noun makes the meaning visible?
4. Could another app use this unchanged?
5. Can we substantiate the promise and its implied scope?
6. Would Josh say it aloud without changing the wording?
7. Does its rhythm differ naturally from neighbouring lines?
8. Can we cut words without losing meaning, warmth, or a necessary limit?

## 5. Specific implications for Cerna

*Everything below is inference from the brief and research; proposed copy requires capability verification.*

**Voice:** an attentive builder who respects your competence. Plain verbs, exact descriptions, occasional dry recognition. Warmth comes from understanding the work. Confidence comes from explaining it.

**Recommended hero direction**

> **Stay on top of your money. Spend less time tracking it.**
>
> Cerna imports your bank statements, removes duplicates, categorises transactions and converts currencies, so you can see where you stand and decide what comes next.
>
> **Join the waitlist**

A more distinctive headline to test:

> **Your spreadsheet has had enough of your weekends.**

Use the first as the clarity baseline. The second assumes a particular maintenance habit and needs testing with intended readers.

“Life is not meant to be lived in spreadsheets” has a recognisable frustration, but broadens it into a life philosophy. Cerna’s sharper position is that financial awareness deserves less maintenance.

**Suggested page sequence**

1. **Hero and waitlist:** outcome, concrete mechanism, explicit prelaunch status.
2. **One annotated product example:** statement → organised transactions → useful overview. Label illustrative data.
3. **The work Cerna takes over:** explain import, duplicate handling, categorisation and currencies through that example.
4. **Privacy explanation:** storage, external processing and owner control in ordinary language.
5. **Short founder note:** a specific personal reason for building Cerna, verified by Josh.
6. **Practical questions and repeated waitlist:** supported formats, platforms, availability, and what happens after signup.

Keep “your finances on autopilot” as a positioning hypothesis. On the page, define its scope as tracking automation: the owner still makes financial decisions.

Next bounded step: verify the capability and privacy claims, then test the two hero directions for comprehension with intended readers. Brand examples inform craft; they do not establish what Cerna’s audience will prefer.
