## 1. Distilled principles

*Principles and examples below are inference for Cerna; linked sources establish the underlying ideas, not their effectiveness with Cerna’s audience.*

1. **Automate upkeep; preserve authorship.** Eight Sleep documents both automatic adjustments and manual control. **Do:** “Cerna handles the tracking. You make the decisions.” **Don’t:** “Let Cerna take over your finances.” [Eight Sleep](https://help.eightsleep.com/en_us/how-to-turn-autopilot-on-off-B19vVtePp)

2. **Make the automation’s boundaries explicit.** Tesla’s documentation distinguishes assistance from autonomy, illustrating how much clarification an expansive name can require. **Do:** “Statement sorting on autopilot.” **Don’t:** leave “Your finances on autopilot” unexplained. [Tesla](https://www.tesla.com/en_GB/support/autopilot)

3. **Sell the attention released.** Amber Case’s calm-technology principles prioritise minimal attention and keeping people in their lives. **Do:** “Less time keeping track.” **Don’t:** “A dashboard you’ll check every day.” [Case](https://calmtech.com/)

4. **Respect the incumbent’s strengths.** April Dunford recommends positioning against the alternatives buyers actually use, including the status quo. **Do:** “The clarity you built your spreadsheet for, with less upkeep.” **Don’t:** “Still stuck in Excel?” [Dunford](https://aprildunford.substack.com/p/a-buyer-centric-approach-to-competitive)

5. **Keep the competent identity intact.** Housel describes how financial strategies can become identities. **Do:** “You already pay attention to your money.” **Don’t:** “Finally get your finances together.” [Housel, 2024](https://collabfund.com/blog/a-few-thoughts-on-spending-money/)

6. **Describe progress beyond the task.** Christensen’s framework asks what customers hire a product to accomplish in their lives. **Do:** “Know where you stand before making your next decision.” **Don’t:** stop at “Automatic categorisation.” [Christensen Institute](https://www.christenseninstitute.org/book/competing-against-luck/)

7. **Find the moment that makes change worthwhile.** Moesta and Spiek connect switching to situational push, attraction, anxiety and habit. **Do:** investigate the last abandoned reconciliation. **Don’t:** assume everyone maintaining a spreadsheet wants to leave it. [Moesta and Spiek](https://jobstobedone.org/radio/pull-and-anxiety/)

8. **Give relief a concrete mechanism.** Things connects mental space with organised tasks. **Do:** “Import statements. Review what needs your judgment.” **Don’t:** “Experience effortless peace of mind.” [Things](https://culturedcode.com/things/)

9. **Make enjoyment legitimate.** Sethi’s conscious-spending approach distinguishes valued spending from spending people care less about. **Do:** “See what you can put towards the things you want.” **Don’t:** “Find out where you’ve been bad.” [Sethi](https://www.iwillteachyoutoberich.com/the-ultimate-guide-to-personal-finance/)

10. **Explain privacy through behaviour.** Apple’s privacy page names processing and sharing boundaries. **Do:** explain what stays locally and what any optional service receives. **Don’t:** substitute “bank-grade” for an explanation. [Apple](https://www.apple.com/privacy/)

11. **Support independence claims with specifics.** Infomaniak explains its independence through ownership and infrastructure choices. **Do:** state relevant, verifiable facts. **Don’t:** imply that “Swiss” alone establishes privacy. [Infomaniak](https://news.infomaniak.com/en/datacenters-independence/)

12. **Be precise without becoming colourless.** Wise’s content designer describes rejecting a voice that played too safe. **Do:** “Your money has enough moving parts.” **Don’t:** “A comprehensive solution for financial management.” [Rachael Blyth, Wise, 2024](https://medium.com/transferwise-design/from-stale-to-stand-out-054aecf95509)

| Headspace: **“Be kind to your mind.”** [Page](https://www.headspace.com/meditation) | Offers care before correction; the person is never the problem. |
| Eight Sleep: **“Room temperature changes. Your sleep doesn’t.”**, beside an adjustment example. [Page](https://www.eightsleep.com/autopilot/) | Names a disturbance, then demonstrates the work absorbed by the product. |
| Oura: sensing → interpretation → guidance. [How it works](https://ouraring.com/how-it-works) | Gives measurement a purpose beyond collecting more data. |

**Inference:** These examples support calm, specific copy. They do not establish a universal “European voice.” Wise’s deliberately energetic approach is a useful counterexample to equating European trust with restraint.

### Six ideas worth testing

1. **Show the owner correcting Cerna.** A short demonstration of an understandable, reversible correction could establish trust better than a flawless animation. Inference from [Eight Sleep’s explicit controls](https://help.eightsleep.com/en_us/how-to-turn-autopilot-on-off-B19vVtePp).

2. **Make the ending uneventful.** Finish the demonstration with a clear financial picture and the person leaving the screen. Treat completion, rather than engagement, as the reward. Inference from [Case](https://calmtech.com/).

3. **Honour the spreadsheet’s original purpose.** “You built it to understand your money.” Then show how Cerna preserves that outcome. Inference from [Dunford’s alternatives-based positioning](https://aprildunford.substack.com/p/a-buyer-centric-approach-to-competitive).

4. **Use ordinary European complexity as recognition.** Illustrate several accounts and CHF/EUR transactions rather than flags or claims about “European DNA.” Inference from [Wise’s practical international positioning](https://owners.wise.com/about-wise).

5. **Distinguish being current from being certain.** A demonstration can visibly separate imported information, proposed categories and owner decisions. Inference from [Apple’s explicit boundaries](https://www.apple.com/privacy/).

6. **Test emotional comprehension before preference.** After showing a headline, ask what Cerna would do automatically and what remains theirs. Inference from [Moesta’s coupled pull and anxiety](https://jobstobedone.org/radio/pull-and-anxiety/).

## 4. Failure modes and “AI-sounding” tells

*Editorial judgments and audience hypotheses below are inference.*

- **Unbounded delegation:** “Forget about your finances.” It could suggest neglect or loss of control. Specify the chores being delegated.
- **Spreadsheet contempt:** “Escape your spreadsheet prison.” It attacks a system the reader may have built with care.
- **Therapeutic overreach:** “Never worry about money again.” Administrative relief cannot establish financial security.
- **Vague reassurance:** “Your trusted financial companion.” Replace the relationship claim with observable behaviour.
- **Generic transformation language:** “Unlock financial freedom,” “supercharge your savings,” “effortlessly optimise your financial journey.”
- **Manufactured intimacy:** “We know how overwhelming money can feel.” Recognise an actual situation instead.
- **Repetitive contrast formulas:** “Less chaos. More clarity. Less stress. More living.” One specific sentence will carry more meaning.
- **European stereotyping:** no evidence here establishes that Swiss readers uniformly dislike exuberance.
- **Invisible import effort:** do not let “autopilot” imply continuous bank synchronisation when statements must be supplied.
- **Privacy absolutes:** do not equate local-first with zero network processing.

These cautions follow the distinction between clear mechanisms and broad promises in [Apple](https://www.apple.com/privacy/), and between useful voice and generic sameness in [Wise](https://medium.com/transferwise-design/from-stale-to-stand-out-054aecf95509).

## 5. Specific implications for Cerna — inference

**Positioning:** Preserve the reader’s competence while removing maintenance. “Autopilot” may evoke both relief and surrender; the sources establish that tension as plausible, not its prevalence among Cerna prospects.

**Recommended hero:**

> **Stay on top of your money.  
> Get on with your life.**
>
> Bring your bank statements. Cerna sorts transactions, spots duplicates and converts currencies, so you can see where you stand and decide what comes next.
>
> **Join the waitlist**

Treat this as candidate copy based on the supplied brief, subject to capability verification.

**Alternative:** “Your tracking on autopilot.” Follow immediately with the specific work automated and the decisions retained.

**Spreadsheet line:** “Your spreadsheet helped you understand your money. Keeping it current became another job.” This acknowledges achievement before friction.

**Single-page sequence:**

1. **Hero and waitlist:** outcome, mechanism, one action.
2. **Product demonstration:** statements become a coherent picture; show an owner decision.
3. **Recognition:** the repeated work of updating, checking and reconciling.
4. **Practical fit:** supported statement formats, accounts and currencies, stated precisely.
5. **Privacy explanation:** storage, optional processing and data ownership.
6. **Founder note and repeated waitlist:** a specific reason for building Cerna, without invented traction or urgency.

**JTBD hypothesis:** When keeping several accounts current starts consuming evenings, help me maintain a financial picture I trust, so I can make decisions and stop thinking about the bookkeeping.

**Switching hypotheses:** Push: reconciliation backlog or another account. Pull: clarity with less maintenance. Anxiety: errors, privacy, losing personal rules. Habit: familiar formulas and an inspectable file. Investigate these through recent real episodes; they are not interview findings.

**Next bounded step:** test three materially different heroes for comprehension and perceived control. Keep “Life is not meant to be lived in spreadsheets” as a supporting sentiment; it leaves too much product explanation to the reader.
