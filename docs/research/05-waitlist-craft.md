## 1. Eleven distilled principles

*Recommendations below are **inferences** from the cited sources, not proven conversion lifts for Cerna.*

1. **Promise a defined next step.** Why: visitors should understand what their email buys them. [Julian Shapiro](https://www.julian.com/guide/startup/landing-pages) connects effective CTAs to the preceding promise. **Do:** “Join the waitlist. We’ll email when you can try Cerna.” **Don’t:** “Unlock your financial future.”

2. **Make the mechanism support the ambition.** Why: specificity gives readers grounds to believe. Shapiro recommends explaining how the headline’s claim becomes possible. [Source](https://www.julian.com/guide/startup/landing-pages). **Do:** name statement imports and currency conversion. **Don’t:** leave “autopilot” unexplained.

3. **Treat waiting as temporary.** Why: interest can decay. Lenny’s founder survey reported lower conversion after longer waits; these are observational benchmarks, not controlled results. [Source](https://www.lennysnewsletter.com/p/what-is-good-waitlist-conversion). **Do:** invite workable cohorts promptly. **Don’t:** accumulate addresses indefinitely.

4. **Gate access for a real operational reason.** Why: restrictions should improve the first experience. Superhuman’s account describes human onboarding and explicitly acknowledges its costs and selection bias. [Source](https://review.firstround.com/superhuman-onboarding-playbook/). **Do:** “We’re inviting people in small groups while we improve imports.” **Don’t:** manufacture exclusivity.

5. **Ask only questions that change an action.** Why: collecting information creates responsibilities as well as learning opportunities. GOV.UK requires a reason and intended use for every question. [Source](https://www.gov.uk/service-manual/design/form-structure). **Do:** ask country when it determines eligibility. **Don’t:** collect bank, income, occupation, and goals “for insights.”

6. **Learn through actual use.** Why: a signup cannot reveal where someone gets stuck. Linear describes refining its product through conversations with early adopters. [Source](https://linear.app/now/practices-for-building-linear-is-now-open-for-all). **Do:** observe a first import. **Don’t:** treat enthusiastic survey answers as product validation.

7. **Referral rewards need real economics.** Why: Harry’s campaign involved tangible products, fulfilment, support, and fraud prevention. [Jeff Raider’s account](https://tim.blog/2014/07/21/harrys-prelaunchr-email/). **Do:** cost any incentive before offering it. **Don’t:** copy a leaderboard because another launch grew quickly.

8. **Start distribution with identifiable people.** Why: personal relevance makes outreach concrete. Justin Jackson describes personal emails that brought Transistor its first ten customers. [Source](https://justinjackson.kit.com/posts/do-this-first-100x). **Do:** identify people already maintaining expense spreadsheets. **Don’t:** begin with “everyone who wants better finances.”

9. **Launch useful improvements repeatedly.** Why: launch need not be one irreversible event. Rand Fishkin identifies smaller feature launches among SparkToro’s useful practices. [Source](https://sparktoro.com/blog/sparktoros-year-one-retrospective/). **Do:** announce newly supported imports to relevant people. **Don’t:** spend every announcement on general excitement.

10. **Explain privacy through boundaries.** Why: concrete exclusions are more inspectable than reassuring adjectives. Rewind’s founder explained local storage and optional external AI processing separately. [Source](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=36877000). **Do:** say what stays local and what gets sent elsewhere. **Don’t:** substitute “privacy-first” for that explanation.

11. **Remember that a waitlist is preparation.** Why: it cannot establish whether the product delivers. Pieter Levels explicitly argues against treating an email form as the product launch. [Source](https://levels.io/bootstrapping/). **Do:** set a bounded path to usable access. **Don’t:** make waitlist growth the destination.

## 3. Six ideas most teams miss

All **inference**, grounded in the linked accounts.

- **Separate learning from admission.** Save the email first, then offer one optional question. This applies [GOV.UK’s question discipline](https://www.gov.uk/service-manual/design/form-structure) without turning registration into research homework.
- **Invite by compatibility rather than influence.** Supported statement format and device can determine readiness; social reach should not. This adapts [Superhuman’s attention to product limitations](https://review.firstround.com/superhuman-onboarding-playbook/).
- **Show the automation boundary.** A small demonstration should include what Cerna handles and what the owner still reviews. Apply [Shapiro’s demonstration guidance](https://www.julian.com/guide/startup/landing-pages) to the potentially ambiguous “autopilot” promise.
- **Make sharing private and uncompetitive.** After signup, offer a plain copyable link: “Know someone maintaining an expense spreadsheet?” Take the sharing simplicity from [Harry’s](https://tim.blog/2014/07/21/harrys-prelaunchr-email/) without its reward ladder.
- **Measure completed first use, not just email capture.** Track confirmed subscriber → invitation → successful import → later return. This follows the emphasis on actual onboarding in [Superhuman’s account](https://review.firstround.com/superhuman-onboarding-playbook/).
- **Use the founder note to explain a product decision.** Explain why Cerna automates bookkeeping while preserving owner judgment. [Rewind’s founding story](https://proxy.rewind.ai/blog/launching-rewind) connects motivation to product direction; Cerna needs only a few sentences.

## 4. Failure modes and AI-sounding tells

These are **editorial inferences**, using [Shapiro’s specificity test](https://www.julian.com/guide/startup/landing-pages) and the operational realities in [Harry’s account](https://tim.blog/2014/07/21/harrys-prelaunchr-email/).

- **Vague transformation:** “Empower your financial journey,” “effortless clarity,” “finally, peace of mind.”
- **Overpromising autonomy:** implying Cerna moves money, imports continuously, or eliminates review.
- **Fake scarcity:** countdowns, invented queue positions, unsupported “limited spots.”
- **Borrowed scale:** treating another founder’s audience, press access, or referral rewards as repeatable conversion evidence.
- **Privacy theatre:** “100% secure,” “GDPR compliant,” or “your data never leaves your device” without substantiation.
- **Founder melodrama:** an origin story longer than the explanation of the product.
- **Unhelpful states:** “Oops!” without recovery, or “You’re in!” when access remains closed.

## 5. Specific implications for Cerna — inference

### Recommended page skeleton

| Section | Word budget | Content |
|---|---:|---|
| Hero and signup | 55–75 | Desired relief, concrete mechanism, waitlist expectation |
| Product demonstration | 25–40 | One real import-to-overview sequence; label sample data |
| What Cerna handles | 60–80 | Imports, duplicates, categories, currencies; owner’s remaining role |
| Privacy | 45–65 | Actual storage and processing boundaries |
| Founder note | 40–60 | Who is building it and the product conviction |
| Practical questions | 90–120 | Availability, supported formats/devices, price, invitation process |
| Repeated waitlist CTA | 15–25 | Same action and promise |
| Footer | Minimal | Contact and privacy notice |

Approximately **330–465 words**, excluding legal text. Repeat the same CTA; avoid a competing newsletter subscription.

### Hero direction

**Recommended:** “Stay on top of your money. Get on with your life.”

**Supporting draft:**  
“Cerna takes care of the tracking: importing bank statements, removing duplicates, categorising spending, and converting currencies. See where your money goes, make adjustments, and get on with your day.”

Publish only capabilities demonstrated in the launch build.

“Life’s too short for financial spreadsheets” is a possible supporting line. The main hero should respect someone who already manages money well. “Your finances on autopilot” needs the mechanism beside it to establish what is being automated.

### Form and microcopy

**Recommendation: email only.** Add a required country question only if it determines access eligibility. Ask about banks during compatibility testing, and spreadsheet pain during research.

I found no primary controlled evidence here establishing the conversion cost of one extra question for this audience. Test **activated users per visitor**, alongside signup rate and learning value.

- **Visible label:** “Email address”
- **Placeholder:** `you@example.com`
- **Button:** “Join the waitlist”
- **Expectation:** “We’ll email when you can try Cerna.”
- **Optional post-signup question:** “What takes the most upkeep?” Choices: categorising, duplicates, currencies, updating the spreadsheet, other.
- **Invalid input:** “Enter an email address, like name@example.com.”
- **Service failure:** “We couldn’t save your email. Please try again.” Preserve the entry.

Visible labels, email autocomplete, and specific errors follow [GOV.UK’s email pattern](https://design-system.service.gov.uk/patterns/email-addresses/).

### Confirmation and the first three emails

**Recommendation: use email confirmation as an address-ownership safeguard, not a conversion trick.** GOV.UK notes that confirmation loops introduce disruption. [Source](https://design-system.service.gov.uk/patterns/email-addresses/)

1. **Immediately — “Confirm your Cerna waitlist signup.”** One confirmation link; explain the requested emails and what happens if ignored. Page state: “Check your inbox to confirm your email.”
2. **After confirmation — “You’re on the Cerna waitlist.”** Thank them, state availability honestly, identify Josh, and offer the optional question. Use a monitored reply address.
3. **When there is substantive progress — “A first look at Cerna.”** Show one working task, disclose a relevant limitation, and explain the next access milestone. If access is ready, replace this with an invitation containing compatibility, price, and setup instructions.

Avoid a timed sequence of empty teasers.

### Timing, price, and privacy

If dates or pricing remain undecided, say so: **“Launch date isn’t set yet”** and **“Pricing isn’t final. We’ll share it before you decide to join.”** Replace these with specifics when decided.

Suggested scope statement: **“By joining, you’re asking for Cerna launch updates and an invitation. Unsubscribe anytime.”** Keep unrelated marketing separate.

Swiss guidance requires informed opt-in for email advertising, sender identification, and easy free opt-out; EU consent guidance requires clear purposes and easy withdrawal. [Swiss authority](https://www.edoeb.admin.ch/de/werbung-marketing), [European Commission](https://commission.europa.eu/law/law-topic/data-protection/information-business-and-organisations/legal-grounds-processing-data_en).

The privacy notice should identify the controller, purposes, recipients, relevant international transfers, retention, and rights. Describe the actual email service separately from local financial storage. [Swiss transparency guidance](https://www.edoeb.admin.ch/en/duty-to-provide-information), [EU guidance](https://europa.eu/youreurope/business/governance-and-sustainability/digital-and-data-compliance/data-protection-gdpr/index_en.htm).

Publish demonstrations around supported workflows; treat Product Hunt as one experiment.
