## Five principles taken

1. **The hero demonstrates; it does not describe** (Wispr Flow, Are.na, Cosmos). Cerna's hero is a statement that tidies itself: raw bank lines become named, deduplicated, CHF-converted, categorised rows.
2. **Say the privacy promise in the business model's terms** (Kagi, Proton, Are.na). Footer note states what is never done with the data, plainly, no badges.
3. **One verb, everywhere** (Wispr Flow, Granola, Kagi). "Join the waitlist" in nav, hero and close; the success state echoes it ("You're on the list").
4. **Lead with a short, opinionated sentence and let typography carry the voice** (Kagi, Notion Calendar's "It's time.", Granola's fragments). The headline is a stance, not a feature list.
5. **Borrow the vernacular of the world you're replacing** (Teenage Engineering's instrument labelling, Lemon Squeezy's index labels, Readwise's typographic play). Cerna's identity device is the spreadsheet itself: grid, cell reference, formula bar. It is present in the hero and fades as you scroll, so the page ends on open paper.

## Lessons rejected

- Logo walls and testimonials: Cerna has none yet, and inventing them breaks "honest over impressive".
- Long FAQ and pricing tables: out of scope for a light waitlist page.
- Certification badges: say what is true in words until there is something to certify.
