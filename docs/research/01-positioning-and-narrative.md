## 1. Twelve distilled principles

*Principles below paraphrase the sources. All do/don’t examples are proposed Cerna copy, marked as inference.*

| Principle | Why and source | Do / don’t — inference |
|---|---|---|
| **Start with the customer’s real alternative.** | Dunford positions against how buyers currently get the job done, including the inconvenience of switching. [Dunford, 2023](https://aprildunford.substack.com/p/a-buyer-centric-approach-to-competitive) | **Do:** “Less time keeping your spreadsheet current.” **Don’t:** “The next generation of finance.” |
| **Own one advantage before listing ten.** | Cohen’s exercise: defend the company through one advantage, then make the pitch, demo and homepage reinforce it. [Cohen, 2010](https://longform.asmartbear.com/one-benefit/) | **Do:** Make reduced upkeep the organising idea. **Don’t:** Give automation, privacy, currencies and insights equal billing. |
| **Choose the person who values that advantage most.** | Vohra used enthusiastic customers’ main benefit to sharpen Superhuman’s audience and priorities. [Vohra, 2018](https://review.firstround.com/how-superhuman-built-an-engine-to-find-product-market-fit/) | **Do:** “Already tracking your expenses?” **Don’t:** “For everyone who wants financial freedom.” |
| **Make positioning answer three questions.** | Kramer reduces the output to who it serves, what it is and why it is better. Research complexity should disappear from the answer. [Kramer, 2024](https://newsletter.mkt1.co/p/positioning-guide) | **Do:** Explain statement-based expense tracking plainly. **Don’t:** Publish the internal positioning workshop. |
| **Find the human meaning inside the product.** | Chesky traced Airbnb’s idea of belonging to early guests’ actual experiences of feeling at home. [Chesky, 2014](https://medium.com/@bchesky/belong-anywhere-ccf42702d010) | **Do:** Connect reliable tracking to getting on with life. **Don’t:** Attach an unrelated life philosophy to a feature list. |
| **Take positions that require a choice.** | Fried and Hansson reject values everyone agrees with; useful positions express practical trade-offs. [REWORK, 2021](https://37signals.com/podcast/mission-statement-impossible/) | **Do:** Explain what remains under the owner’s control. **Don’t:** “We believe in innovation, trust and excellence.” |
| **Let conviction supply the personality.** | Fried describes good marketing as the transfer of enthusiasm, including the care embedded in something that simply works. [Fried, 2025](https://world.hey.com/jason/marketing-is-8d39f651) | **Do:** Explain why one irritating task deserved fixing. **Don’t:** Add jokes to manufacture a “human brand.” |
| **Use ordinary language as a truth test.** | Graham argues simple writing lowers reading effort and exposes weak ideas. [Graham, 2021](https://paulgraham.com/simply.html?viewfullsite=1) | **Do:** “See where your money went.” **Don’t:** “Unlock actionable financial clarity.” |
| **Describe the annoyance that prompted the product.** | Dinh’s launch account begins with concrete frustrations and explains what he built in response. [Dinh, 2023](https://news.tonydinh.com/p/making-22k-in-7-days-the-story) | **Do:** Describe repeatedly fixing categories or currency formulas, if true. **Don’t:** Invent a grand founder epiphany. |
| **Respect how customers see themselves.** | Barry says “ConvertKit” put off some creators who understood their work as providing value rather than converting fans. [Barry, 2025](https://nathanbarry.com/2024-review/) | **Do:** Address capable people tired of maintenance. **Don’t:** Imply they need rescuing from financial incompetence. |
| **Show precision through small details.** | Collison ties Stripe’s emphasis on craftsmanship and precision to the consequences of getting details wrong. [Collison interview, 2024](https://newsroom.haas.berkeley.edu/stripe-co-founder-and-ceo-patrick-collison-on-founding-a-company-that-should-have-already-existed/) | **Do:** Define exactly what “automatic” covers. **Don’t:** “Everything handled. Effortlessly.” |
| **Give money a purpose beyond optimisation.** | Sethi’s voice makes room for personally meaningful spending and challenges small-purchase guilt. Study the rhetorical permission, rather than importing his financial advice. [Sethi](https://www.iwillteachyoutoberich.com/the-ultimate-guide-to-personal-finance/) | **Do:** “Know where you stand. Choose what matters.” **Don’t:** Treat every discretionary purchase as a failure. |

## 2. Concrete examples collected

*The wording and page structures are observed; explanations of their mechanisms are inference, not measured conversion findings.*

| Real example | Mechanism — inference |
|---|---|
| **“Belong Anywhere” — [Brian Chesky](https://medium.com/@bchesky/belong-anywhere-ccf42702d010)** | A functional transaction acquires a human purpose. The accompanying story supplies experiences that make the abstraction understandable. |
| **“Screen emails like you screen your calls” — [HEY](https://www.hey.com/)** | A familiar behaviour explains an unfamiliar feature. The visitor understands the mechanism before learning the product terminology. |
| **“Ship your startup in days, not weeks” — [ShipFast](https://shipfa.st/)** | A desired outcome meets a concrete alternative. The page then itemises repetitive setup work and includes Marc’s first-person origin story. Borrow that specificity; Cerna needs its own quieter delivery. |
| **“Just start typing” — [Tally](https://tally.so/)** | A small, familiar action makes starting feel manageable. The surrounding explanation compares building a form to writing a document. |
| **“Easy to use and privacy-friendly Google Analytics alternative” — [Plausible](https://plausible.io/)** | Category, comparator and distinguishing attributes appear together. Supporting sections explain what privacy means rather than leaving it as a badge. |
| **“Super simple group chat, without a subscription. And you get the code, too.” — [ONCE](https://once.com/)** | Plain product description plus a consequential commercial choice. The conversational final sentence carries personality without a separate brand manifesto. |

## 3. Six less-obvious opportunities

All are **inference**, grounded in the linked sources.

1. **Make the spreadsheet evidence of competence.**  
   It demonstrates that the visitor already cares. Position Cerna as preserving that care while removing repetitive work. This applies Barry’s lesson about respecting customer identity and Dunford’s treatment of the existing solution. [Barry](https://nathanbarry.com/2024-review/), [Dunford](https://aprildunford.substack.com/p/a-buyer-centric-approach-to-competitive)

2. **Make a finished task the emotional climax.**  
   Show statements becoming an understandable picture, followed by a clear stopping point. The desired experience is permission to close the app. This extends Cohen’s single-advantage discipline. [Cohen](https://longform.asmartbear.com/one-benefit/)

3. **Use a truthful limitation as brand voice.**  
   Explain where automation ends and owner judgment begins. A precise boundary could express “has your back” more convincingly than reassurance adjectives. [Collison](https://newsroom.haas.berkeley.edu/stripe-co-founder-and-ceo-patrick-collison-on-founding-a-company-that-should-have-already-existed/), [REWORK](https://37signals.com/podcast/mission-statement-impossible/)

4. **Give every warm claim a concrete companion.**  
   Follow “less upkeep” with an actual import example. Follow “private by default” with an accurate explanation of storage and external processing. Plausible provides an inspectable example of supporting its privacy positioning with specifics. [Plausible](https://plausible.io/)

5. **Let readers recognise the founder’s standard of care.**  
   A short note explaining one stubborn problem and the decision made to solve it can reveal character. It needn’t become a founder biography or revenue story. [Dinh](https://news.tonydinh.com/p/making-22k-in-7-days-the-story), [Fried](https://world.hey.com/jason/marketing-is-8d39f651)

6. **Test whether someone can recommend Cerna in one sentence.**  
   After reading, ask them to explain it to a friend. Listen for the intended benefit without supplying the vocabulary. Graham explicitly stresses descriptions that survive being retold. This tests comprehension, not product demand. [Graham](https://www.paulgraham.com/convince.html?viewfullsite=1)

## 4. Failure modes and “AI-sounding” tells

These are **editorial judgments and inference**, not reliable tests of AI authorship.

- **Interchangeable aspiration:** “Empower your financial journey.” Apply Graham’s plain-language standard: what happens, for whom? [Graham](https://paulgraham.com/simply.html?viewfullsite=1)
- **A villain that insults the customer:** spreadsheet users have invested effort in their system. Challenge its maintenance burden. [Dunford](https://aprildunford.substack.com/p/a-buyer-centric-approach-to-competitive)
- **Personality performed through punctuation:** relentless fragments, exclamation marks and forced banter. Fried’s alternative is actual enthusiasm and care. [Fried](https://world.hey.com/jason/marketing-is-8d39f651)
- **Unopposable values:** “calm, trusted, intelligent.” Name the decisions those values produce. [REWORK](https://37signals.com/podcast/mission-statement-impossible/)
- **Emotional copy hiding the category:** visitors should understand the product before interpreting its worldview. [Kramer](https://newsletter.mkt1.co/p/positioning-guide)
- **Automation without boundaries:** “Never think about money again” makes a larger promise than administrative automation. Precision should govern the claim. [Collison](https://newsroom.haas.berkeley.edu/stripe-co-founder-and-ceo-patrick-collison-on-founding-a-company-that-should-have-already-existed/)
- **Borrowed founder swagger:** copying ShipFast’s urgency and income language would import its personality, not establish Cerna’s. [ShipFast](https://shipfa.st/)

## 5. Specific implications for Cerna — inference

**The idea to explore:** dependable financial tracking with less upkeep.

**The enemy:** repeatedly maintaining the picture of your finances.

**The narrative:** You already make the effort to understand your money. Keeping the records current consumes attention. Cerna handles the repetitive tracking work, leaving you to make the decisions and return to your life.

“Life is not meant to be lived in spreadsheets” contains the right emotional direction. It also risks dismissing a tool this audience chose deliberately. Keep the release from upkeep; lose the judgment.

### Proposed hero

**Your money, without the spreadsheet upkeep.**

Import your bank statements. Cerna categorises transactions, catches duplicates and converts currencies, so you can see where you stand and decide what comes next.

**Join the waitlist**

This is candidate copy based on the brief, not a verification of launch capabilities. “Your finances on autopilot” needs qualification: **tracking on autopilot** is the more precise territory.

### Proposed single-page sequence

1. **Promise and waitlist:** headline, literal explanation, email field.
2. **Recognisable situation:** a brief account of keeping categories, imports and currencies current.
3. **Product evidence:** one real, redacted or clearly labelled demonstration of the repetitive work disappearing.
4. **Control and privacy:** explain what Cerna handles, what needs review, and what local-first means in practice.
5. **Founder note:** one concrete reason for building it and one meaningful product choice.
6. **Repeat the waitlist:** explain what signing up leads to, once that process is decided.

**Voice:** a capable person quietly taking care of tedious work. Warmth comes from noticing the details; trust comes from accurate promises.
