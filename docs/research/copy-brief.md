# Cerna landing page: copy brief (shared by all proposers)

## What Cerna is, and what you may claim
Cerna is a personal finance product for people in Europe (first user is Swiss, CHF-based). TRUE today and safe to state:
- The owner imports bank statements they download themselves (UBS PDF, Revolut CSV today). There is NO live bank connection. Never imply continuous sync, "connect your bank", or that Cerna moves money.
- Cerna reads the statement, deduplicates overlapping exports, converts every currency into the owner's home currency for reporting, and pre-selects a category for each transaction.
- The owner confirms categories (one row or a visible batch). Cerna never finalises a category without confirmation; it suggests, the owner decides.
- Home gives a calm overview: where the money went this month, what is usual for you, what changed. Money kept is shown as the space left over.
- Local-first today: the owner's data stays on their own machine. (Do not claim "never leaves your device", "GDPR compliant", "bank-grade", or anything about future hosting.)
DO NOT invent: launch date, price, country list beyond "Switzerland/Europe" as intent, founder biography, customer numbers, testimonials, business model claims (ads/selling data), AI chat, investing, budgeting envelopes, forecasts.
Pre-launch status: the only action is joining a waitlist for an invitation.

## The reader
Already on top of their money. Often keeps an Excel/Sheets file for expenses, built with care, now a chore: fixing a currency formula, re-pasting a statement, a duplicate row, categories drifting. The file is useful but approximate or too time-consuming. Competent, a little proud of the sheet, allergic to hype and to being treated as a beginner. Wants something like Monarch but is not in the US/Canada. Brand feeling to deliver: it has your back; it understands you. Calm, trustworthy, human, precise.

## Owner's seed (rough, to be improved, not to be preserved)
"Life is not meant to be lived in spreadsheets." Josh feels it is convoluted. The research says it also risks mocking a tool this reader chose on purpose. The idea underneath is good: automate the tracking so you can course-correct, save, and go back to living; for people who care about being in control but want their time back. The user's pitch word is "autopilot" ("having your finances on autopilot"); research says it must be bounded (tracking on autopilot; the owner stays the decider) or it suggests losing control.

## Principles distilled from six research runs (follow these)
1. Position against the real alternative (the spreadsheet and its upkeep), not against "the old way". Own ONE advantage: the upkeep goes away, the control stays.
2. Respect the reader's competence. The spreadsheet is evidence they care. Never "finally get your finances together", never "escape spreadsheet prison".
3. Lead with what the reader keeps/gets (on top of it, time back), then the literal mechanism in the same breath. A stranger must know what Cerna does within 5 seconds.
4. Ordinary words, spoken rhythm, concrete nouns (statement, duplicate, francs, euros). Every line passes: could another app use this unchanged? If yes, rewrite.
5. Show the boundary: what Cerna does, what you still decide. That precision IS the "has your back" feeling, more than any reassurance adjective.
6. Give each warm claim a concrete companion (a duplicate caught, EUR 18.40 becoming CHF 17.21).
7. Privacy as behaviour (what stays where), not a badge. Only claim what is true.
8. Humour optional and dry; never joke about spending or financial competence. One memorable observation beats forced quirkiness.
9. Hero length: headline about 10-14 words at most, supporting copy 25-40 words. Whole page roughly 330-470 words excluding legal. One CTA repeated, same words. Page may end early.
10. Waitlist microcopy is honest: label "Email address", button "Join the waitlist" (or better), say what happens next ("We'll email when you can try Cerna"), no fake scarcity, no "You're in!" when access is closed, specific error states.

## Ban list (reject on sight)
seamless, effortless, frictionless, unlock, elevate, empower, revolutionise, supercharge, powerful, intelligent, cutting-edge, next-generation, financial wellness/journey/freedom, "Meet your new financial best friend", "Never worry about money again", "not just X, but Y", "It isn't X. It's Y.", manufactured triplets ("Less chaos. More clarity. More living."), em-dash asides as a tic, rhetorical question openers, "In a world where", "Say goodbye to", "Welcome to the future of". Plain verbs only.

## Editor's checklist applied to every line
What does it help the reader understand or do? Who acts and what happens? Which concrete noun makes it visible? Could another app use it unchanged? Can we substantiate it? Would a person say it aloud? Does its rhythm differ from the neighbour? Can we cut words without losing meaning or a necessary limit?

## Deliverable (max 900 words, Markdown, final message; also write it to the file path you are given)
1. YOUR LENS in one line and the one user moment you serve.
2. TEN hero headline options (with a one-line subhead each for your top 3), spanning genuinely different angles (outcome, reframe, recognition, mechanism, time, identity, restraint). Mark your top 3 and say why in one line each. Each headline max 14 words. No two may share a structure.
3. For your #1 hero: full page copy within the word budget: hero + subhead + CTA label + the line under the button; three beats (each: a 3-6 word title and one sentence containing a concrete detail); a privacy line (only true claims); a founder-note STUB written as a template with [bracketed blanks] for facts only Josh can supply (do not invent a story); closing line + CTA; success state; error states (invalid email, service failure).
4. How you would bound or replace the word "autopilot" on the page.
5. Three lines you wrote and then cut, and why (shows your editing).
6. One risk in your own top pick.
Write like a person. Run the ban list and checklist on your own output before sending.
